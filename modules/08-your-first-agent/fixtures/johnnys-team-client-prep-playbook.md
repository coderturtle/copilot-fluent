# Johnny's Team: Client Prep Playbook (Internal Only)

*Simulation only. Kellerman & Castle, Johnny Castle, Sam Wheat, Oda Mae Brown, Wade Garrett, and every
name, step, and rule below are fictional, at a fictional firm operating in no real regulatory regime;
nothing here is calibrated to one. None of this is a real client, a real account, or real financial,
legal, or accounting guidance.* <!-- brand-lint-ignore: disclaimer naming what this fixture is not, per docs/brand.md's escape hatch for stating a rule by naming the phrase it excludes -->

**What this document is for.** This is Johnny's team's own internal reference for getting ready
before a client review meeting - nothing in it is written for a client to read, and no version of it
ever goes to one. It exists so whoever's covering a meeting-prep task this week doesn't have to
re-learn the team's own conventions from scratch or guess at them.

## Standing rule: the packet order

Every client-prep packet Johnny's team assembles before a client review meeting follows the same
three-part order, in this order, every time:

1. **Household Snapshot** - every linked account under one household, current values only, no
   commentary.
2. **Open Follow-Ups** - anything promised at the last meeting that isn't closed yet, and who owns
   finishing it.
3. **Meeting Agenda Draft** - a short list of what this particular meeting needs to cover, in the
   order Johnny wants to raise it.

For a household with more than one linked account, the Household Snapshot has to be generated **at
least 2 business days before the meeting** - not pulled together the morning of.

## Who to loop in for what (internal routing only)

- A data pull from the ledger is missing or looks off: check with Sam Wheat before assuming the
  numbers are final.
- The meeting needs a Copilot Page built or shared afterward: that's Oda Mae Brown's part of this,
  same as it is for any Page in this workshop.
- A client's older holdings need context that isn't in the current file: ask Wade Garrett before
  assuming there's nothing on record - he covered a lot of these relationships for years.

## What this playbook is not

This is a meeting-prep checklist for the team's own use, not guidance for a client - nothing in it
says or implies what any client should do with their own money, and it isn't a stand-in for anyone
reviewing anything on the firm's behalf. It only covers how the team gets itself organized before a
meeting; it was never meant to cover anything past that.

## A closing reminder for whoever's prepping

This is scratch material for internal use before a meeting. It isn't a script, and a client should
never see a version of it. If a copy of this ever ends up in front of a client, that's a mistake to
fix, not a new use for it.
