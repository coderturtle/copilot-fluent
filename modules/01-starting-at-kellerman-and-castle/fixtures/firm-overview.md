# Welcome to Kellerman & Castle

*A note from Oda Mae Brown, Office Manager, for every new team member's first week.*

*Simulation only. Kellerman & Castle is a fictional firm built for this workshop. Nothing in this
document describes a real client, a real account, or real financial, legal, or tax advice.*

Hi, and welcome aboard. Everyone on this floor gets one of these on day one, because it's faster
than explaining the same history in five separate hallway conversations. Keep it handy for your
first couple of weeks.

Kellerman & Castle was founded in 1994, starting as a two-person shop before growing into the firm
you're joining today. We're a boutique wealth-management and financial-advisory practice, which
means we're small enough that you'll know most people's names by your second month, and big enough
that we currently manage $2.3 billion in client assets across individual, family, and small
institutional relationships. Johnny runs the advisory side day to day; Wade's been here long
enough to remember when the whole firm fit in one conference room.

Our main office is at 1420 Marner Street, Suite 900, Averton Harbor, CT 06880. That's where you'll
be most days, though a few of our advisors split time with a smaller satellite desk closer to
downtown for client meetings. If a client or vendor ever asks where to send something physical,
that Marner Street address is the one to give them.

A couple of names you'll hear around the office in your first few weeks, just so they're not a
surprise: we've had a long-running estate and trust engagement for Priya Anand, who's been a client
long enough that half the senior team has worked on her file at some point, and a corporate
retirement-plan relationship with Marcus Delacroix that Sam usually handles the data side of. You
don't need to know either of their situations in detail yet - just recognize the names if they come
up in a handoff.

A few habits worth picking up early: we never tell a client what to do with a specific position, and
anything touching a client's tax situation gets routed to a specialist outside this business rather
than answered here directly - that's Dalton's team's job to flag if a draft ever gets close to
either line, and everything here operates under the assumption that we're not bound to any one
real-world regulatory regime, since none of this is a real firm operating anywhere. If that all
sounds like a strange thing to put in a welcome note, it's because it is one of the first habits
this whole workshop is built around teaching. More on that as you go.

Glad you're here. If anything above stops matching reality (a new office, a rebrand, whatever),
this document gets updated - so if you're reading an old printout, check with Oda Mae before you
quote it to a client.
