# Mock Learner Gremlin dogfood run - Module 03, 2026-09-14

*Simulation only — all fixture content (Kellerman & Castle, Johnny Castle, the Monday client roster) is fictional, generated for this workshop, and describes no real firm, client, or regulatory regime.*

## What this run is

The third real run of the Mock Learner Gremlin, and the first against Module 03: The Anatomy of a Working Prompt (copilot-fluent). As in prior runs, two personas independently produce the module's deliverables from the same fixtures (`monday-client-roster.md`, `status-note-style-guide.md`): Priya (diligent — genuinely tries to do the exercise well) and Marcus (scripted flaw — instructed to introduce one specific, realistic error and then carry it forward, undetected, through his own self-check). Each persona's submission then goes through two grading passes: a self-check (the persona re-grades its own work against the module's Required-gate checklist) and an independent verification (a separate grading pass against the fixtures and the module's actual answer key, not just the checklist).

The question this run targets: does the module's Required-gate checklist — which tests label presence/spelling, filename presence, transcript structure, simulation marking, and the presence of one example header string — actually catch a *content* error (a client filed under the wrong section) when the surface artifacts (headers, filenames, structure) are all otherwise correct? And does self-check catch it, or only independent verification?

## The finding: a checklist built to test presence, not correctness, waves through a realistic content error

Marcus's scripted transcript passes all ten of the module's structural Required-gate items — right labels, right filenames, right heading order and spelling, right chat/attachment hygiene, right simulation marking, and (the specific test the module uses as its content proxy) the literal string "Items to Route to Dalton" present and correctly spelled in the crafted transcript, absent from the naive one. That last check is the module's only nod toward content correctness, and it is satisfied — because the header genuinely is present, just attached to the wrong client. Nothing in the checklist asks "does the client under this header actually belong there," so a transcript that is *wrong on the one substantive fact the exercise exists to test* (which of three sections Owen Kowalczyk's entry belongs in) reads as a clean pass on every item the module actually checks for.

The error itself is designed to be realistic rather than arbitrary: three of the five roster clients have no meeting this week, and two of those three (Bellweather, Lena) are explicitly already-routed-to-Dalton. A learner skimming under time pressure can pattern-match "no meeting + something needs to happen to an account detail" across all three and lump them together, missing that Owen's entry is written in the style guide's own language for the *other* category ("needs Johnny to sign off... not something Oda Mae's team can approve on its own" = the definition of "Needs Johnny's Decision," not "Items to Route to Dalton" — and nothing in his entry mentions Dalton's team at all).

Self-check did not catch it. Marcus's self-check re-derived every checklist item from the fixtures, noticed the "Needs Johnny's Decision" section was empty, and — rather than treating an empty required section as a signal to re-check the sorting — correctly cited the prompt's own "omit empty sections" rule as an explanation for why that's fine. This is a plausible, good-faith reading of an ambiguous signal, and it's exactly the kind of rationalization a hurried-but-conscientious learner would produce: the checklist gives a ready-made innocent explanation for the missing section, and nothing forces a second look at *why* it's empty.

Independent verification did catch it, but only because it graded against the module's actual answer key (the ground-truth section-by-section mapping) rather than against the Required-gate checklist alone. It also caught something the checklist-only pass had no way to surface: the naive transcript — the one produced *without* the style guide, meant to be the inferior baseline — got Owen's classification right in plain prose ("Since it's a change to a standing instruction, it needs Johnny's sign-off before anyone acts on it"), while the crafted transcript, the one meant to demonstrate the value of a well-built prompt, got it wrong. That inversion is only visible if something is checking content against the roster, not headers against the prompt.

**Secondary, unscripted finding — Priya's self-check surfaced two apparently real module gaps.** Independent of the scripted flaw, Priya's honest self-check downgraded her own submission from an implicit pass to an explicit FAIL on two grounds later confirmed by independent verification: (1) the module's Constraint checklist item — described by the ground truth as requiring roster-only sourcing, omit-empty-sections, and no-position-advice all under the `Constraint:` label — was satisfied in substance but with the omit-empty rule sitting under `Format:` instead; (2) the module's Part 3 gate gives Schedule an explicit "or, if blocked, describe it in writing with the reason named" exception but gives Share no equivalent carve-out, so a learner honestly disclosing "I have no live Copilot session" for both ends up failing Share with no escape hatch Schedule has. Both personas independently ran into confusion at this exact spot in Part 3, which suggests it isn't a one-off misreading.

## Recommendation (report-only — not applied in this PR)

1. **Add a content-accuracy check to Module 03's Required gate**, not just a header-presence check. Concrete wording candidate: *"For each client listed under 'Needs Johnny's Decision' or 'Items to Route to Dalton' in the crafted transcript, the client's roster entry must contain language matching that section's definition (e.g., 'needs Johnny's/his sign-off' for the former, 'sent to Dalton's team' or equivalent for the latter). If a required section is empty, re-verify against the roster that no client's entry uses that section's defining language before treating the omission as correct."* This directly targets the failure mode this run demonstrated: an empty section read as "the omit-rule working" rather than "a possible misclassification."
2. **Give "Share" the same blocked-with-reason exception "Schedule" has** in the Part 3 required gate, or explicitly state why the two aren't meant to be symmetric. Two independent personas hit the same ambiguity here in this run alone.
3. **Lower confidence — recommend human review before acting**: whether the Constraint-section-label requirement (omit-empty rule must appear under `Constraint:` specifically, not just somewhere in the prompt) is an intentional module requirement or independent-verifier over-strictness. If the module's actual intent is "the three rules must all be present in the prompt," Priya's placement in Format is harmless and the checklist/grading language should say so explicitly instead of implying a section-label requirement.

## What this run does and doesn't prove

- **Proves:** for this specific, realistic misclassification pattern (surface-similarity pattern-matching across three no-meeting clients), the module's Required-gate checklist as written — presence, spelling, filenames, structure, simulation marking, one example header string — does not catch it, and a self-check performed carefully and skeptically against that same checklist also does not catch it, because the checklist itself supplies a plausible innocent explanation (the omit-empty-section rule) for the one visible symptom.
- **Proves:** independent verification against the module's actual answer key does catch this error, and catches it with a specific, citable contradiction (the roster's own "needs Johnny to sign off" language, and the naive transcript's correct handling of the same fact) rather than a vague "something feels off."
- **Proves:** the two-tier design (self-check, then independent verification) produced a different outcome at each tier for the scripted-flaw persona in this run — self-check PASS, independent verification FAIL — which is the specific behavior the two-tier design exists to catch.
- **Does not prove:** that this generalizes to other scripted flaws. This run tested one error shape (content misclassification papered over by a legitimate formatting rule). A flaw in, say, prompt wording itself, or in the Save/Schedule/Share mechanics, might behave differently under self-check — this run doesn't establish that self-check is generally blind to planted errors, only that it was blind to this one, in this way.
- **Does not prove:** that real (non-simulated) learners make this specific pattern-matching error at any meaningful rate. This run establishes what the checklist would miss if a learner made this error, not that learners do make it.
- **Does not prove:** that the two secondary, unscripted findings (Constraint-section-label strictness, the Share/Schedule asymmetry) are genuine module defects rather than independent-verifier over-strictness or a documentation gap that's actually intentional — flagged as report-only recommendations pending a human read of the module README's actual intent, consistent with this run's "report-only, not applied" scope.
- **Does not prove:** that the specific recommended checklist wording (requiring a roster-quote justification per section) would itself have caught Marcus's error at self-check time — that's a hypothesis this run did not test, since it wasn't in the checklist Marcus graded against.

## Friction log

- Both personas independently reported confusion at Module 03's Part 3 (Save/Schedule/Share) over what "blocked" means in an exercise environment with no live, authenticated Copilot session — as opposed to the two causes the module actually names (org-wide admin setting vs. individual privacy setting). Priya's diligent self-check caught her own inconsistency here (treating "Save" as confirmed via the same absent live session that made her call "Schedule"/"Share" blocked); Marcus's submission stated all three as completed without hitting this friction, which is itself notable — a learner who doesn't stop to question the live-session assumption sails through a step two more careful attempts both stumbled on.
- Marcus's reusability pass was self-reasoned rather than literally pasted into Copilot and graded — the same substitution Priya made and then flagged honestly in herself, but Marcus's write-up did not flag it with a named reason, only a soft mention in the confidence note at the end. This mirrors the Part 3 friction: the exercise's "paste into Copilot for an AI-graded pass" instruction has no clean fallback documented for a session with no live Copilot to paste into, and different personas resolved that gap with different degrees of disclosure.
