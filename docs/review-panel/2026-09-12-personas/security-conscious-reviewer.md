# Security-Conscious Reviewer — Review of `docs/workshop-design.md`

**Top finding — §12's honesty flag is not wired to anything.** The doc admits the DLP/Purview claims are unverified and says "re-verify... before any of this reaches learner-facing content," but nothing in §7/§8/§13 tracks this as a blocking dependency. Contrast with `docs/brand.md`, which every Tier-1 prohibited-language check explicitly marks "[not yet executable]" pending Phase 3. §12 gets a prose caveat but no equivalent gate. If Coachgremlin's content pass treats prose notes as advisory rather than blocking, unverified compliance claims about how Copilot protects data ship as fact. Recommend: add §12's re-verification to `docs/next-actions.md` as a named blocker, same tier as the maintainer-preflight item already there.

**Module 02 injection mitigation is a design intent, not a tested control (§8).** Delimiter blocks + "treat delimited content as data" is a known-fragile defense against a category of attacks specifically chosen for this module because it's adversarial. The doc names the risk honestly but defers the actual mitigation to content time with no stated validation step (e.g., "before shipping, confirm the grading prompt survives N known injection patterns"). Worth adding that as an explicit Phase-3/content-time acceptance criterion, not just a described approach.

**Same-AI-grades-own-work (Tier 2) is disclosed as circular but not exploited pedagogically.** §8's self-critique is genuinely strong — better than most designs get. But the doc misses the obvious teaching moment: Module 02 teaches over-reliance (1.2.1) while the whole workshop's grading mechanism *is* an instance of over-reliance. Nothing prompts the learner to notice this. A one-line callout would convert a design liability into reinforcement of the exam's own lesson.

**Financial-data framing (§5) mitigates liability, not habit-formation.** Simulation marking and no-real-identifiers rules protect against compliance/legal exposure, but Module 02's "what wouldn't you paste in a real scenario" answer is only presence-checked at Tier 1 — its content isn't required to actually land §12's point ("enterprise licence ≠ safe to paste"). Recommend binding that Tier-2 rubric explicitly to §12's teaching point rather than leaving it generically graded.

Overall: unusually self-aware design; these are closable gaps, not fundamental flaws.
